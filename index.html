What is breadth-first search doing?

Breadth-first search (BFS) explores the grid in “rings” around the start: first every cell 1 step away, then every cell 2 steps away, and so on. Because it always expands in this order, the first time it reaches the target it must have found a fewest-steps route — that is why BFS gives the shortest path on an unweighted grid.

Watch the frontier: it spreads like water from a tap. Cells are coloured as they join the queue (frontier) and darken once they are expanded. When the target is reached, the algorithm walks the recorded parent links backwards to reveal the path.

The comparison runs both sorting algorithms on the same random arrays, counts every elementary step, and measures real elapsed time. Results are averaged across trials. Try growing the size 2× and watch how each column scales.
